# "Your Teeth When You Sip Soda All Day": script v1

**Core fact:** acid wears enamel away, and **how often** your teeth get acid matters more than how much soda you drink. Every sip drops the mouth's pH into the zone where enamel softens. Saliva neutralises the acid and helps repair early softening, but only if it gets a break between sips. Enamel that's worn away doesn't grow back. Diet soda is acidic too.
**Takeaway:** drink it with a meal instead of sipping all day, then rinse with water.
**Length:** about 40 s · 1080×1920 · the same cut-away cartoon style as The Gap.

## Fact check (wording kept honest)
- **Soda is acidic, including diet:** colas are around pH 2.5–3.5 and contain phosphoric and/or citric acid. Enamel starts to soften (demineralise) below roughly pH 5.5. True as said ("Still acid").
- **Bacteria turn sugar into acid:** that's the "burp" metaphor. True.
- **Frequency matters:** each sip restarts the acid attack, and saliva needs time between attacks to neutralise and repair early softening. "Sipping all day" is worse than one quick drink. True.
- **Saliva repairs early softening, but can't regrow lost enamel:** "patch you up" refers to early softening only. "Won't grow back" is true for worn-away enamel.
- **Advice:** "Drink with meals, then rinse with water" is standard, harmless advice. No product claims, nothing beyond general dental advice.
