# Script playbook: tooth & body animation shorts

What I learned from studying:
- **The reference short** (LowkeyLitStudio stomach video), watched frame by frame with the Watch plugin.
- **18 top videos** across three groups: organ comedy, tooth animation, and real dentist explainers.
- **141 search results**: titles, lengths and view counts.

On the evidence: YouTube blocks transcripts from the cloud machine, so for the 18 videos I studied titles, view counts and four stills each, not their dialogue. The reference was studied in full.

## 1. What the winners have in common

| Video (views) | What it does that works |
|---|---|
| **Organ Story** (108M) | Opens **outside** (a man eating), then cuts **inside** to the organs suffering. One everyday trigger, an inside reaction. |
| **CasiCreativo "Your Organs When/During…"** (4–16M each) | One everyday trigger per video (pregnancy, sugar, fasting, running). Full-body organ characters with arms and legs; each organ takes a turn reacting. The thumbnail is the title in big text plus the cast grouped together. |
| **Organ Drama** (100–170K) | Gadgets that **count** the escalation (a speech bubble "2"… "5" pizza slices, a timer, a red alarm button). Props on the characters (a nightcap on the stomach, a heart lifting weights). |
| **Organismo** (515K) | Organs in human situations (the brain gaming in sunglasses). The punchline cuts back to the human outside. |
| **"Bad tooth" cartoons** (75M and 8.5M) | **No dialogue at all**, so they work in every country. Teeth are rival characters: the cool gold-crowned tooth in sunglasses vs the jealous decayed one. The dentist's tools come in from above like a threat. |
| **TED-Ed cavities** (7.7M) | Builds a metaphor world (bacteria living in houses and shops on the tooth) to explain the mechanism. |
| **Dr. Binocs / StoryBots** (3–5M) | A host character plus a cute villain germ. Healthy teeth wear sunglasses: "cool tooth" is an instant visual shorthand. |
| **Dentalism** (40M) | Realistic close-ups with a **red arrow** on the exact problem. Clarity beats decoration. |
| **Real dentists on "not replacing a missing tooth"** (0.3–1.8M) | The same topic as The Gap. Their thumbnails use a shocked face, a red arrow and 3–5 words ("WHAT DO I DO NOW!?"). Their 3D clips show neighbours **tipping into the gap** with arrows, so the image is proven to land. |
| **Science Insider braces** (6.2M) | Leads with a counter-intuitive fact: "BRACES DISSOLVE YOUR JAWBONE". |
| **Reference stomach short** | Constant chatter (about 3 words/s, one word per caption), a **"20 YEARS LATER"** black card between act 1 (cause) and act 2 (consequence), a **split screen** of the brain control room over the organ, a **gauge** (blood sugar) that turns red, a static camera with big characters, and a watermark. |

**Title patterns in this sample:**
- **"Your Organs When/During…"**: 28 videos, median **2.4M** views.
- **Emoji in the title**: median about 1.7M.
- **Plain "Why/How…?" questions**: median about 38K.
- **Lengths:** 2–3 minute videos had the highest median in this sample. Treat that loosely: the search surfaced more long-form uploads than Shorts.

## 2. The formula

**PREMISE = an everyday trigger + an inside view + one surprising fact.**
"Your teeth when you lose one" beats "how teeth move". Write the title first; if it isn't a "your X when Y" or a shocking fact, the idea isn't sharp yet.

**Cast (3–5 characters, never more on screen at once):**
- **The victim**: the one the trigger happens to.
- **The boss**: the brain, a control room or a gauge. Explains, panics, and talks to the camera.
- **The opportunist or villain**: wants something (the neighbour teeth want the space).
- **The comic relief**: a running gag (the tongue can't stop poking).

Each needs **one want plus one look prop** (sunglasses, hard hat, ice pack, nightcap). If two characters want the same thing, merge them.

**Structure for a 35–45 s Short** (scale the timings up for 2–3 minutes):

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **HOOK** | Start mid-crisis with sound (a scream, an alarm). Never start with an explanation. |
| 2–8 s | **SETUP** | Who's hurting and why, in at most 2 lines. A label on each new character. |
| 8–18 s | **TRIGGER** | The big physical event (the extraction). Funniest line here. |
| 18–24 s | **INTERACTIVE** | A guess, quiz or poll ("Who moves in first?"). A countdown and a comment prompt. |
| 24–26 s | **TIME JUMP** | A full-screen card ("6 MONTHS LATER"). It resets attention like a scene change. |
| 26–36 s | **CONSEQUENCE** | Escalate 3 times, each worse than the last. The fact is delivered by a character, not a narrator. |
| 36–40 s | **PAYOFF** | Callback to the hook or running gag (the bonk, the tongue poke), then the thesis in 4–5 words. |
| end | **LOOP** | End on a reaction face or a cliffhanger so the replay feels natural. |

## 3. Dialogue rules

1. **One breath per line:** 3–8 words. If a line needs a comma, split it between two characters.
2. **Every line has an attitude** (scared, smug, bossy, clueless). Delete any line a narrator could have said.
3. **Show the fact through a character's want.** Write "Free real estate!", not "the teeth drift into the space".
4. **Rule of three for escalation**, with the third beat the biggest (tip → lean → over-erupt).
5. **One running gag, three hits:** set it up, repeat it, then twist it at the end.
6. **Break the fourth wall once**, for the interactive beat ("Comment your guess!").
7. **Shouts and gasps are lines too** ("AAAAAH!", "WHAT?!", "OW!"). They give the voice actor energy and give the captions punch.
8. **Avoid captions that are lone weak words.** With one-word captions, group "I LOVED IT!" and "ALL OF THEM!".
9. **No medical advice beyond "ask your dentist".** Treatment depends on the person.

## 4. Accuracy rules (an educational channel lives on trust)

- **One core fact per video,** verified in a primary or professional source before writing.
- **Soften numbers honestly:** "up to half", never an absolute.
- **Keep the jokes, not the falsehoods:** exaggerating speed is fine with a time card, but inventing biology isn't (teeth don't "grow", they move).
- **Words commenters will correct:** hardness ≠ toughness, "can't heal" ≠ "can't be repaired", "dead tissue" ≈ no living cells.

## 5. Writing for the visuals

- **Write actions, not descriptions:** "the forceps clamp, wiggle twice, POP" is something an animator can time.
- **Every scene needs one visual change every 2–3 s:** a label, a prop, an expression, a counter or a cut.
- **Show the mechanism once, unmistakably:** an arrow, an X-ray close-up or a gauge (the reference's blood-sugar meter, Dentalism's red arrows).
- **Gadgets make escalation readable:** counters, gauges, alarms, a brain-room screen. They must tell the truth (red while something is wrong).
- **Props say personality instantly:** sunglasses = cool and healthy, hard hat = builders, ice pack = hurting.
- **The thumbnail frame is planned in the script:** one shocked face, one red arrow, and 3–5 words.

## 6. Script template

```
TITLE:        Your ___ When ___   (or: a shocking-fact headline)
CORE FACT:    ___ (source: ___)
CAST:         victim / boss / opportunist / comic relief - want + prop each
HOOK  0-2s:   [sound] + line (<=6 words, mid-crisis)
SETUP 2-8s:   line, line   [labels: ___]
TRIGGER:      action beats (clamp -> struggle -> POP) + funniest line
INTERACTIVE:  question to camera + countdown + "Comment ___"
TIME CARD:    "___ LATER"
CONSEQUENCE:  beat 1 -> beat 2 -> beat 3 (each a character line + a visual)
PAYOFF:       callback to the gag + thesis (<=5 words)
END:          reaction face (loops into the hook)
THUMBNAIL:    face ___ + arrow at ___ + text "___"
```

## 7. Checklist before recording voices

- [ ] Title is "your X when Y" or a shocking fact.
- [ ] Hook is mid-crisis, with sound in the first second.
- [ ] Every line is 3–8 words and has an attitude.
- [ ] One running gag, hit 3 times.
- [ ] Escalation in threes.
- [ ] One interactive beat with a comment prompt.
- [ ] Time-jump card between cause and consequence.
- [ ] Core fact verified, numbers softened.
- [ ] Ends on a reaction or callback, not a lecture.
- [ ] A visual change every 2–3 s is written into the action lines.

## 8. "The Gap" against this playbook

**Already strong:**
- The hook is the scream, then Brain's alert.
- Clear cast wants: Righty wants space, Lefty is "just leaning", Upper is lonely.
- Running gag (Tongue) and an interactive quiz.
- "6 MONTHS LATER" card, rule-of-three consequences, and the bonk callback.
- One core fact, worded honestly.

**Upgrades for the next script pass:**
1. **Open outside, then go inside** (Organ Story's trick): about 1 s of a person biting ice ("crunch"), then the scream inside. It shows the cause in one shot.
2. **Give the "cool" neighbour a sunglasses prop** before the extraction (the trope in the 75M-view tooth cartoon), then make it lose the glasses when it tips over: a visual joke on top of the fact.
3. **Add one counter or gauge** the brain room watches: "GAP: 1 tooth" → "TEETH TILTED: 3" → "BONE: -50%". It turns the consequence into escalating numbers, like Organ Drama's slice counter and the reference's sugar meter.
4. **Hit the Tongue gag three times:** poke → poke → at the end, poke the brain-bonk bump.
5. **Thumbnail line in the script:** Upper's shocked face + a red arrow at the gap + "YOUR TEETH MOVE?!".
